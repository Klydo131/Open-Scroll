# Why the templates look the way they do

Open Scroll's templates are starting points drawn from published practice. Every church changes them, and Open Scroll is built so it can: parts can be renamed, reordered or removed, any part of the day can be saved as the church's own template, and the names of things can be changed in Settings.

The sources below are mostly Seventh-day Adventist, because the Adventist pattern (Sabbath School, the Divine Service, and evangelistic series) is the most fully documented in public. The general templates follow the shape common to most Protestant churches.

## Sabbath School

- **The usual order.** A song service, opening hymn and prayer, a welcome, a mission story, special music, then lesson study from the quarterly Bible study guide, closing with a hymn and prayer.
- **Sabbath School Alive!** This guide from the church's Sabbath School department builds the hour around Bible study and prayer, fellowship and mission. It suggests a mission segment with a world mission report, a local outreach story and personal ministries training. That is the second Sabbath School template.
- **A quarter at a time.** Superintendents plan the programs for a whole quarter, with the secretary keeping attendance and ordering the study guides. The quarter usually ends with a Thirteenth Sabbath program. That is why Advanced can plan up to 26 weeks at once and make the last one a Thirteenth Sabbath program.
- **Thirteenth Sabbath.** Since 1912 the last Sabbath of each quarter has carried a special mission offering. Part of it goes to named projects in one world division, so the program usually features that field.

Sources: [Sabbath School (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbath_school); [Sabbath School Net](https://ssnet.org/); [Sabbath School & Personal Ministries](https://alive.adventist.org/); [Adult Sabbath School coordinator description](https://discipleship.adventistchurch.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2022/03/24-Sabbath-School-Coordinator.pdf); [Sabbath School and mission offerings](https://stewardship.adventistchurch.com/sabbath-school-offerings/); [13th Sabbath Offering](https://www.askanadventistfriend.com/sabbath-meaning/13th-sabbath-offering/).

## The Divine Service

- **The longer order of worship.** The *Church Manual* and the *Elder's Handbook* give a longer and a shorter order. The template follows the longer one:
  - call to worship and invocation
  - hymn and scripture reading
  - pastoral prayer
  - tithes and offering
  - children's story and special music
  - sermon
  - closing hymn and benediction
- **Communion.** The Communion service is usually held once a quarter. The ordinance of humility (foot washing) comes first, then the Lord's Supper with unleavened bread and unfermented grape juice. Communion is open to all believers. The general Communion template follows the words of institution in 1 Corinthians 11.

Sources: [Seventh-day Adventist Church Manual, 2000 edition (archives)](https://documents.adventistarchives.org/resources/churchmanuals/cm2000.pdf); [Fundamental Belief 16: The Lord's Supper](https://adventist.org/beliefs/official/the-lords-supper).

## Evangelistic series

- **Shape.** Meetings run nightly or three to five nights a week, for three to four weeks. A prayer team meets before each night.
- **Each night.** A typical night has a song service and a health moment, then a children's story and special music. Next comes a message of about 45 minutes ending with an appeal, then the next night's topic.
- **Topic order.** The topics usually build from trust in the Bible and the person of Christ, through the commandments and the Sabbath, to the church and baptism. The built-in prophecy series follows that order.
- **Follow-up.** Interest cards and decision cards have long been how a series knows whom to visit. Ministry writers stress that the level of interest should set how soon someone is visited, and that the goal is disciples rather than decisions.
- **What Open Scroll keeps.** A series in Advanced keeps only nightly numbers: present, guests, interest cards and decisions. Names and addresses of guests belong in the church's own secure records, not in a planning tool on a shared device.

Sources: [Ministry, "Subjects and Organization" (1960)](https://www.ministrymagazine.org/archive/1960/03/subjects-and-organization); [Ministry, "Use of Evangelistic Cards" (1940)](https://www.ministrymagazine.org/archive/1940/06/use-of-evangelistic-cards); [Ministry, "The Evangelistic Aftermeeting" (1946)](https://www.ministrymagazine.org/archive/1946/10/titleless); [Ministry, "The Uniform File System Concluded" (1969)](https://www.ministrymagazine.org/archive/1969/06/the-uniform-file-system-concluded).

## What is deliberately left out

- **Hymn titles and numbers.** Hymnals belong to their publishers, and every church sings its own. Templates say "Hymn number" as a hint and leave the rest to the church.
- **Lesson titles.** The quarterly study guides are published under their own terms. The church types this week's title itself.
- **Names of people in templates.** A saved template keeps the order and minutes, never who served.
